Changing Up The Blog

AI slop everywhere, including here. No matter where you look the text and the code are all written by AI. The sad part about it is that SOTA models like 5.5 opus are writing paragraphs and python modules better than us, thats a hard reality for me to swallow. As someone that has built my career around being good at writing code, it is hard to accept that the robots are better than me... but that is the truth. Calling it AI slop seems disingenuous at this point. Today it is just AI generated code and text.

At work, I used to think of myself as the type of employee who was very good at creating and maintaining code while maybe not being the best at communicating with my peers, directing projects, or engaging with end users to define requirements. The problem today is that creating and maintaining code is no longer a differentiating skill, it is a comodity that you trade tokens for. So now I must, we as software engineers must, differentiate ourselves by defining what software systems need to be created and how they need to be created. That means leveraging domain knowledge, brainstorming with peers, and understanding end users to define goals for LLMs to execute on.

That does not mean that I am useless, right? ... right?

Safe Spaces

AI will be writing most of my code and it will be clogging up your Facebook feeds, that is why I wanted to make a change in this blog. It seems increasingly important to me that we have places to go on the internet where you can have some guarantee that the content was generated by a human. So from this time forward all the content in these blogs will be generated by me which will also explain any grammatical or spelling errors. I am deleting all posts before this date (well this is tracked in a public github repo so you can track those al posts generated with AI assistance if you so desire). AI will still be used for putting the site together, formatting my css, even potentially for research and pulling toghether data that I will use in my posts, but in the end all of the thoughts and the words typed will be mine.

Why I Think Writing May Make Me Better

The strain required to craft a clear memo or report is the mental equivalent of a gym workout by an athlete - Cal Newport

It feels like we may be losing our ability to think as well as we used to by relying on AI all the time. I think that most people are aware of this to varying degrees at this point. We are becoming less able to maintain focus for extended periods of time. Writing a 200 character professional email all by yourself feels like a significant task. Its like having a bowl of candy sitting on your office desk. If you do so then you will certainly eat more candy than you otherwise would and that will likely lead to a caloric surplus in your diet. Same with AI. With claude-code sitting open in a tmux pane, why not just ask it to clean up that email for you? After some time this will eat at your ability to write a nice email yourself.

Losing your ability to write professional emails may be just fine for you, personally I do not care all that much, but eating at my ability to write all together seems like a problem. Not only would I like to maintain and even increase my capacity to write clear text I think that writing is something more. It is our way of consolidating our thoughts and carefully crafting them in a way that another human being (or bot, I suppose) can understand what is going on in our heads. From my perspective, losing your ability to read and write is essentially the same as losing your ability to think.
